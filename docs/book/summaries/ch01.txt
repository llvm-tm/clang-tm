PURPOSE: Pure-motivation chapter (no TM exposition): shared memory's history
sets the stage, then locks are shown to fail at composition. The "innocent
change" that introduces a second lock and inverts lock order is the book's
founding bug; the bank transfer enters here as the running example.

SECTIONS
- Why Synchronization Became a First-Class Problem
    Three eras (distributed -> DSM -> multicore/synchronization era); how
    each left its stamp on coordination problems. (History prose; sets up
    Ch.2's own telling.)
- The Cost of Mixing Synchronization with Logic
    Locking crisis: critical sections as a second language; deadlock,
    priority inversion, lost wake-up, data races, Heisenbugs.
- Composability -- The "Innocent Change" Problem
    Lock-order obligations are not in interfaces; two correct components
    deadlock when composed. The thesis of the chapter.
- Running Example: The Innocent Change in Code
    Bank transfer: global-lock version (lst:transfer-basic), the innocent
    two-lock change with ordering obligation (lst:transfer-safe), same logic
    as a transaction (lst:transfer-atomic).
- Worked Example: Reproducing the Two-Lock Deadlock
    Compilable pthread deadlock (lst:two-lock-deadlock); the deadlock comes
    from opposite orders in transfer_safe vs audit, not either section alone.
- Worked Example: Model Checking the Bank Invariant
    BankAtomic TLA+ model (lst:bank-atomic): money conservation + floor
    without mutexes or lock order.
